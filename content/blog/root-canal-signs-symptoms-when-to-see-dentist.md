# How to Tell If You Need a Root Canal: Signs, Symptoms and What to Do

Tooth pain can make it hard to focus on anything else. If you have a toothache that will not go away, you might wonder if you need a root canal. The words “root canal” can sound intimidating, but the truth is that this treatment can save your tooth and stop the pain for good. In this post, we will cover the signs you might need a root canal, what the process involves, and what steps to take if you think you need one.

## What Is a Root Canal?

A root canal is a dental procedure that treats infection or inflammation inside the tooth. Each tooth has a soft core called pulp, which contains nerves and blood vessels. If the pulp gets infected or damaged, it can cause pain, swelling, and even lead to tooth loss. A root canal removes the damaged pulp, cleans out the inside of the tooth, and seals it to prevent further problems.

## Why Do People Need Root Canals?

Root canals are needed when bacteria get inside the tooth, usually from a deep cavity, a cracked tooth, or an old filling that has failed. Sometimes, trauma to the tooth, even if there is no visible crack, can cause the pulp to die and become infected. If not treated, the infection can spread and cause serious health problems.

## Signs You Might Need a Root Canal

Not all tooth pain means you need a root canal. Here are the most common warning signs:

### 1. Persistent Pain

If you have a toothache that does not go away, especially when you bite down or put pressure on the tooth, you could have an infection inside the tooth.

### 2. Sensitivity to Hot or Cold

Does your tooth hurt when you drink coffee or eat ice cream? If the pain lingers for more than a few seconds after contact with hot or cold, this could be a sign of nerve damage.

### 3. Swollen Gums

Swelling near a sore tooth, especially if it comes and goes or forms a small bump (an abscess), may mean you have an infection that needs treatment.

### 4. Tooth Discoloration

A tooth that turns gray or dark compared to your other teeth may have a dying nerve inside. This is often a sign that the pulp is damaged.

### 5. Pain When Chewing

If it hurts to chew or touch the tooth, especially if the pain is sharp or throbbing, you may need a root canal.

### 6. A Chipped or Cracked Tooth

A visible crack or chip can let bacteria into the pulp, leading to infection. Even if you do not feel pain right away, a root canal may be needed to prevent future problems.

### 7. Pimple on the Gums

A small, recurring bump on the gums near a painful tooth can be a sign of an abscess. This is your body’s way of trying to drain the infection.

## What Happens During a Root Canal?

Root canals sound scarier than they are. Here is what you can expect if your dentist recommends one:

1. **Numbing the Area:** The dentist will use local anesthesia to make sure you do not feel pain during the procedure.
2. **Removing the Infected Pulp:** A small opening is made in the tooth, and the infected or dead pulp is removed.
3. **Cleaning and Shaping:** The inside of the tooth is cleaned and shaped to get rid of bacteria.
4. **Filling and Sealing:** The cleaned space is filled with a rubber-like material, and the tooth is sealed to keep bacteria out.
5. **Restoring the Tooth:** Often, a crown is placed over the tooth to protect it and restore its strength.

Most people say the procedure feels similar to getting a filling. The tooth and surrounding area might be sore for a few days, but the severe pain should be gone.

## What If I Ignore the Symptoms?

Ignoring signs of infection can lead to bigger problems. The infection can spread to your jaw, other teeth, or even other parts of your body. You might end up losing the tooth or needing more complicated treatment. If you notice any of the symptoms above, it is best to see a dentist right away.

## Steps to Take If You Think You Need a Root Canal

If you have tooth pain or any of the symptoms listed, here is what you should do:

### 1. Call Your Dentist

Do not wait for the pain to get worse. Call your dentist and describe your symptoms. If you do not have dental insurance, some offices offer [special pricing for exams and x-rays](https://www.reddydentalfl.com/offers/no-insurance/).

### 2. Take Care of Your Tooth

While you wait for your appointment, avoid chewing on the sore side. Rinse your mouth with warm salt water to reduce swelling. Over-the-counter pain relievers can help, but do not put aspirin directly on your gums.

### 3. Keep the Area Clean

Brush and floss gently around the sore tooth. Keeping your mouth clean can help control infection.

### 4. Watch for Changes

If you notice swelling that spreads, fever, or trouble swallowing, call your dentist right away. These can be signs of a serious infection.

## Preventing Root Canals

Good dental habits can lower your risk of needing a root canal:

- Brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste.
- Floss daily to remove plaque between teeth.
- Get regular dental checkups and cleanings.
- Address cavities and broken teeth as soon as possible.

## Checklist: What to Watch For

Here is a quick checklist to help you decide if you need to call your dentist:

- Tooth pain that will not go away
- Sensitivity to hot or cold that lingers
- Swelling or a bump on your gums
- Tooth that is darker than your other teeth
- Pain when chewing or touching the tooth
- Chipped or cracked tooth

If you check any of these boxes, it is time to make an appointment.

## What to Expect After a Root Canal

Most people feel back to normal within a few days. Mild soreness is common, but severe pain is not. Your dentist may recommend a crown to protect the tooth. With good care, a tooth that has had a root canal can last as long as your other teeth.
